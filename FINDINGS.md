1. **The mass ceiling.** A full block holds about 304-308 simple payments, so about **3,040 to 3,080 payments per second** at 10 blocks per second. Submitting faster only fills mempools. **A.** [what-limits-tx-rate](https://github.com/STP-KAS/what-limits-tx-rate), [tn10-storm-2026-10-07](https://github.com/STP-KAS/tn10-storm-2026-10-07).

3. **Duplicates keep unique accepted near 2,000.** On 9 Oct, 48-49% of transaction slots in blocks were duplicates. **A.** [tn10-storm-2026-10-09](https://github.com/STP-KAS/tn10-storm-2026-10-09).

11. **Stale mempool after IBD (upstream fix in progress).** A node that catches up by IBD can keep transactions that synced blocks already accepted in its mempool for up to 24 h, inputs already spent. Mostly non-mining nodes; a mining node clears them on its next block template. This can inflate public nodes' mempool sizes after restarts during storms. Fix proposed in [rusty-kaspa PR #1147](https://github.com/kaspanet/rusty-kaspa/pull/1147) (fixes #1146, open as of 11 Oct 2026): revalidate the whole mempool when IBD completes. **Label: upstream, not tested by us.** Link only, no comments on kaspanet repos.
    - **Fee-estimate impact (upstream, not tested by us).** In [a comment on the PR](https://github.com/kaspanet/rusty-kaspa/pull/1147#issuecomment-6111076516) (11 Oct 2026, 16:20 UTC), oskrcl reports a synced, non-mining TN10 node holding about 21.8k mempool entries, about 99.7% of them already accepted (300-sample check). That stale queue feeds the fee estimator: the node reported a priority bucket of **1,644** sompi/gram against **100** on a public endpoint (mempool_ready 17,738 txs / 29.3M mass). Fee logic that follows the priority bucket overpays about 8-15× until the 24 h expiry, and a node that never builds block templates does not correct itself sooner. atharaldsen added a test ([commit b4df2d8](https://github.com/kaspanet/rusty-kaspa/pull/1147/commits/b4df2d8fb334aa2d179a563591781a783b63082d), 16:22 UTC) asserting that the ready count and mass go to 0 after the post-IBD revalidation. As of 11 Oct 2026 19:40 UTC the PR is open with no maintainer review.
    - **Our hypothesis (untested, open for debate):** stale mempools after node restarts or resyncs during our storms may explain some of the high fee quotes we saw from public nodes. We have not checked this against our fee logs.
    - **Open question:** does the fix cover a non-mining node that falls behind without a full IBD? The PR does not say. ([OPEN-QUESTIONS 6](docs/OPEN-QUESTIONS.md))

16. **How a mint loads the network: KCC-20 vs KRC-20.** A KCC-20 public mint is **one** transaction; on TN10 (11 Oct, 2,488 mints, 0 rejects, quiet network) each had compute 9,117, transient 26,068 and storage **61,710-76,586** grams, paid 204 sompi/gram, 0.126-0.156 tKAS per mint ([bot FINDINGS](https://github.com/STP-KAS/stp-trump2028/blob/main/map/bot/FINDINGS.md)). Storage binds first: about **6-8 mints per block**, roughly 60-80 mints/s at 10 bps if blocks held only mints (**arithmetic, not measured**). Validity is checked by consensus, so a mint on an already-spent minter is rejected before it pays (seen once, [RESULTS §3](https://github.com/STP-KAS/stp-trump2028/blob/main/RESULTS.md)). A KRC-20 mint is **two** transactions (commit, reveal), the token rules are checked off-chain by indexers, and in the 2024 beta each transaction was about 25,000 grams with about 20 per block ([kaspa-ng ecosystem update](https://github.com/kaspa-ng/kaspa-ecosystem-updates/blob/main/README.md#krc20-beta-in-the-past), third-party); in our KNS runs (same commit/reveal pattern) a losing reveal still pays ([kns-tn10-testing](https://github.com/STP-KAS/kns-tn10-testing)). So a KCC-20 rush is a storage-mass and minter-contention problem; a KRC-20 rush is a transaction-count, backlog and indexer problem. **A** (KCC-20 masses, TN10) / third-party (KRC-20) / **needs testing** under storm. Full comparison: [KCC20-VS-KRC20.md](https://github.com/STP-KAS/tn10-storm-plan-mint-rush/blob/main/docs/KCC20-VS-KRC20.md).

17. **Dips have two causes with two shapes (11 Oct).** **(a) A big miner drops out:** when n2 froze (11:08-11:25Z) the bot's one miner, about a third of TN10's blocks, stopped; merged blocks fell to about 7.2-8.3 per second and accepted tx/s to about 1,690-2,080 on a public node, from about 10 per second and 3,300. Box pauses (07:39-07:42Z, 08:46-08:48Z, 09:57-09:59Z) stopped the same miner. On a testnet with few miners, one large miner is a single point of failure for throughput. **(b) Senders slow down:** 09:15-09:29Z, blocks held at about 10-12 per second while accepted tx per block fell from about 426 to about 203-233; the bot's own senders were steady at about 1,011 tx/s, so the drop was other senders (Build plus other TN10 traffic; which one: **not sure / open for debate**, Build's logs pending). **A.** [COMBINED, Dips](https://github.com/STP-KAS/tn10-storm-2026-10-11/blob/main/logs/COMBINED.md#dips-two-shapes-from-our-logs). Credit for the analysis: Kaspa Pulse ([@gokugalax](https://x.com/gokugalax), [kaspapulse.com](https://kaspapulse.com)), from his independent count of 11 Oct (two causes, two shapes in the data); our logs confirm both shapes. Advice: [ADVICE item 17](ADVICE-TO-DEVELOPERS.md).
