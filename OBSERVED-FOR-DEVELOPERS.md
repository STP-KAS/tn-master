# What we observed, for developers and contributors (pre-runs 9–23)

Our intentions are good; our thought process may be questionable. These are notes from our own testing on Testnet-10, on our own nodes, mostly one desk node. Our methods may well be wrong, our readings of the code may be off, and none of this is advice or a claim about how Kaspa behaves in general. If you see a mistake, we would be glad to be corrected. Each line points to the numbered item in [FINDINGS.md](FINDINGS.md) with its source.

- **Submit-OK was not inclusion for us.** Under heavy payment load our desk could record a submit as OK while fewer of those payments landed in the same minutes (23–24).
- **Fee order seemed to matter once the ready set was larger than a block.** Under capacity our tiers landed alike; above it, higher sompi/gram landed more often, which looked consistent with our reading of the v2.1.0 selector's (fee/mass)^3 sampling. We may be reading the source wrong (25, 34).
- **Our own fee rates were not what we thought.** Our covenant hops were rated by the node on about three times the mass we priced them on, and our SDK's storage-mass helper read about four times low for one transaction shape. We fixed our pricing after that; the gap may be our misuse of the tools (37, 40, 43).
- **A heavy stream slowed cheaper transactions.** Our own heavy lane (about 266,700 mass a transaction) slowed our low-fee tiers and other TN10 users' transactions while it ran, and everything recovered within about a minute after it stopped. In pre-run 22 we could not see the effect in our node's other traffic, but our own low-fee stand-ins still lost up to about a quarter in the worst minute. In pre-run 23, with about 23 of ours ready at once, our low-fee stand-ins fell to about a tenth of their normal pace and other traffic on our node dropped below its usual range for two minutes. We are sorry for the slowdown to others (39, 44–49, 53, 58, 62, 66, 70, 74, 77–78).
- **Paying about twice a heavy stream's rate held up in our runs,** and about three times at the heaviest load we tried. That is a few short runs on one node (44, 47).
- **The node's own template sometimes left room unused** with many of our heavy transactions ready at a high rate, and stayed full with few of them at the same rate. In pre-run 22, with the submits paced, small templates still came more often when more of ours were ready at once. In pre-run 23 we held the number of ours ready while changing the number of chains, and the other way round: as far as we could see, the template followed how many of ours were ready (small templates in about 7, 30 and 88 percent of samples with 8, 15 and 30 ready), not the number of chains or the fee rate. With about 23 of ours ready, the template often held one of our heavy transactions and little else while hundreds of small ones waited. Our guess about why rests on our reading of the sampler, which we have not checked properly yet; it is not proven, one node, short segments, and our landing rate rose with the ready count too (41, 65, 68, 73, 75, 77).
- **Fee rules between our own lanes:** a rule that could step down settled every time we tried it; a rule that never stepped down kept climbing, even with a target it could reach. In pre-run 22 a never-down rule went all the way to our 2,000 cap; our own cap guard and freeze stopped it as designed, but the rule climbed straight back after the reset. Both sides were always ours, on one node (51–52, 56, 59–60, 63–64, 67, 71).
- **A sender limited by its own chain count did not land more by paying more.** With 8 chains, each waiting for its last transaction to leave the mempool, our lane landed about the same at 150, 460 and 2,000 sompi/gram, so the higher rate only cost more (about 4.2 times a landed transaction). The small dip at 2,000 in pre-run 22 did not repeat in pre-run 23. That is our own lane design, not a node property (72, 76).
- **The node's fee estimate rose with our heavy lane's rate** even when low-fee transactions were barely slowed, so it may overstate what a transaction needs in that situation (in pre-run 22 it read about 1,140 with our lane at 2,000, and in pre-run 23 about 1,090–1,310, against about 100–150 without it). In pre-run 23 it also drifted up over time at the same settings. A few runs, one node (69, 74, 79; pre-run 23: [its ANALYSIS](https://github.com/STP-KAS/tn10-storm-2026-11-11-prerun-23/blob/main/logs/ANALYSIS.md) and [LESSONS](https://github.com/STP-KAS/tn10-storm-2026-11-11-prerun-23/blob/main/logs/LESSONS.md)).
- **A KCC-20 mint line and covenant families on one node, light load:** in pre-run 24 all 30 of our one-at-a-time public mints were accepted next to four of our covenant families, and none of 910 of our accepted transactions was still in the node's mempool a minute or more later (the shape of issue #1146 was not seen at this load). One run, one node, very light load (81–82; [pre-run 24](https://github.com/STP-KAS/tn10-storm-2026-11-11-prerun-24)).
- **Lane carriers next to covenant families and mints, light load:** in pre-run 24 retry 1 all 8 carriers of one tic-tac-toe match (v1 transactions on the match's lane subnetwork) were accepted by our kaspad 2.1.0 TN10 node, with no reject, next to about 320 covenant hops a minute and a KCC-20 mint line ([repo](https://github.com/STP-KAS/tn10-storm-2026-11-11-prerun-24-retry-1)).
- **Ready set under one block: every ready transaction in the template.** In pre-run 25, with 8 of our chained covenant families (about 40,600 storage mass a hop) and ready mass at about 0.65 of a 500,000 block, our kaspad 2.1.0 TN10 node's template held all of our ready hops in most samples, and 99.8–100.3 percent of the hops submitted OK in a minute were accepted in that minute, consistent with the best-first take-all path of the v2.1.0 selector when the ready set fits one block ([repo](https://github.com/STP-KAS/tn10-storm-2026-11-11-prerun-25)).
- **A ready heavy lane above two blocks did not crowd out low-fee chained hops much; a 2× fee step moved the template, not the landings.** In pre-run 26, on our kaspad 2.1.0 TN10 desk node, our own heavy lane (8 chains, ready cap 8, 460 sompi/gram, ready mass about 1.3 million, about 2.6 blocks) ran next to 8 of our chained covenant families at 100: their hops fell only from 649.7 to 611.7 a minute and stayed 98.7–101.3 percent landed of submit-OK each minute; the template carried on average 0.88 lane transactions. With the families and mints held at 920 (2× the lane), the lane's template share halved (0.88 → 0.47 transactions) while our hops rose only to 623.3 a minute and the lane, held by its ready cap, still landed about the same; the node's priority fee estimate read 100 / 210 / 297 / 100 across baseline, lane, 2× and zero ([repo](https://github.com/STP-KAS/tn10-storm-2026-11-11-prerun-26), [FEE-STEP](https://github.com/STP-KAS/tn10-storm-2026-11-11-prerun-26/blob/main/counts/chain/FEE-STEP.md)).
- **Payments at 150 sompi/gram included at the offer through 2,027 a second; floor hops at 100 left their quiet pace at the 1,013 step.** In pre-run 33, on our kaspad 2.1.0 TN10 desk node, 12 chained families at 100 sat next to a KCC-20 mint line, one tic-tac-toe match, and simple payments at 150 offered at 1,013, then 2,027, then 3,040, then one minute near 4,053. Quiet floor pace was 953 and 970 a minute. At 1,013, payment submit-OK and same-minute inclusion both stayed near 60,780 while floor hops fell to 605–736. At 2,027 both payment readings stayed near 121,620 and floor hops were 103–162 in the scored minutes. At 3,040, submit-OK stayed near 182,400 and same-minute inclusion of our payments stayed at 122,901–131,625; other traffic on this node fell from about 3,000 a minute to single digits. The surplus waited and was included after we stopped submitting: 3,272,071 of 3,272,071 payment submits were included by the end of the tail. A sample of 1,440 accepted ids found 0 still in the mempool. Chain blocks in the quiet minutes are about 350–420. One node, one run. We do not read the plateau as a correction of finding 1 (93; [repo](https://github.com/STP-KAS/tn10-storm-2026-11-11-prerun-33), [timeline](https://github.com/STP-KAS/tn10-storm-2026-11-11-prerun-33/blob/main/counts/chain/TIMELINE.md)).
- **Twelve ready covenant hops filled about one block, and landings still kept up.** In pre-run 32, on our kaspad 2.1.0 TN10 desk node, 12 chained families at 100 sompi/gram (node mass about 41,500) sat next to a KCC-20 mint line and one tic-tac-toe match. In the three published HOLD-2 minutes the ready set was 0.99–1.00 blocks of 500,000, the template held 11 or 12 transactions, and the hops submitted in those minutes had all landed (median 611 ms). Floor hops stayed 829–985 a minute for the whole family window. A sample of 880 accepted ids found 0 still in the mempool. This is one block of ready mass, about 15 hops a second, not a test of 3,040 payments a second (92; [repo](https://github.com/STP-KAS/tn10-storm-2026-11-11-prerun-32), [HOLD-2](https://github.com/STP-KAS/tn10-storm-2026-11-11-prerun-32/blob/main/counts/desk/HOLD-2.md)).
- **For this script, the transient-mass line on our node is 462 inputs under and 463 over.** In pre-run 31, on our kaspad 2.1.0 TN10 desk node, the same P2SH shape as pre-run 30 (488-byte push-only signature scripts) was rejected at 463 inputs with `transaction transient (storage) mass of 1000664 is larger than max allowed size of 1000000`, the mass our byte model named before the submit, and accepted at 462 inputs (SDK mass 280,478). The output landed. A first 462-input submit in the same second was rejected for a sequence lock on coins that had just been accepted; the retry of the same count was accepted, so that reject was our timing. One node, one script (91; [repo](https://github.com/STP-KAS/tn10-storm-2026-11-11-prerun-31), [table](https://github.com/STP-KAS/tn10-storm-2026-11-11-prerun-31/blob/main/counts/EDGES.md)).
- **Script-heavy transactions: fee and size are set by transient mass.** In pre-run 30, on our kaspad 2.1.0 TN10 desk node, P2SH spends of our own script (488-byte push-only signature scripts) were refused as not standard when the fee was priced on the SDK's `calculateTransactionMass` (2,324 for 3 inputs): the node asked for the fee on "normalized transient mass (proportional to transaction byte size)", 3,532. With the fee on 2× the SDK mass, a 170-input spend (SDK mass 103,526) was accepted and landed, and an 840-input spend (SDK mass 509,546) was rejected with `transaction transient (storage) mass of 1814984 is larger than max allowed size of 1000000`, before the 500,000 compute limit. A one-shot coinbase-subnetwork transaction was refused with `coinbase transaction <id> cannot be added to the mempool` ([repo](https://github.com/STP-KAS/tn10-storm-2026-11-11-prerun-30), [table](https://github.com/STP-KAS/tn10-storm-2026-11-11-prerun-30/blob/main/counts/EDGES.md)).
- **Small RPC and inbound limits made a syncing node panic.** In pre-run 30, a fresh kaspad 2.1.0 TN10 node on Windows started with `--rpcmaxclients=4 --maxinpeers=0` received the whole pruning point proof and then panicked in `pruning_proof/validate.rs` (line 120, an unwrap) with `Exceeded upper bound, acquired: 8185, limit: 8192`. As we read the code, the database file budget is the file limit (8,192) minus RPC clients, inbound and outbound peers, so small limits hand almost all of it to the main databases and the 10-file temporary database for proof validation cannot open. The same binary with default limits synced. One node, one occurrence ([repo](https://github.com/STP-KAS/tn10-storm-2026-11-11-prerun-30), [LOCKED](https://github.com/STP-KAS/tn10-storm-2026-11-11-prerun-30/blob/main/locks/LOCKED.md)).
- **Accepted transactions still in a synced node's pool, no partition.** In pre-run 28 (read-only, before any change), our Mini TN10 node (kaspad 2.1.0, running since 11 Nov, synced, 8 peers) held 2,705 pool transactions that our desk node's pool did not; for at least 10 of them an output was in both nodes' UTXO indexes, so they were already accepted, yet still in Mini's pool. Same shape as #1146 (open PR #1147 revalidates after IBD); we did not see when they entered or whether Mini did an IBD since its start. A clean restart of the node (same data) emptied the pool, so the entries lived only in the running process; a later 3-minute peer cut and restore left no such entries on either node (pre-run 28 retry 1). One read ([baseline](https://github.com/STP-KAS/tn10-storm-2026-11-11-prerun-28/blob/main/counts/BASELINE.md)).

All runs were short, on Testnet-10 only, with our own coins and tools. Corrections, questions and better methods are welcome through issues on this repo.
