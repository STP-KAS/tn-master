# Playbook

Who does what, and the gates before each machine may start. A pre-run's own plan, lock, and go files, when it has them, are not overridden by this page.

## Oversight

TN10 ops oversees this rehearsal end to end: Mini, the desk (Build), and the bot. TN10 only. Never mainnet. Do not edit the Tuesday repo, the Wednesday repo, or a closed pre-run's go files. Do not write `REVIEW_CLOSED`.

The operator's start is the arm. From pre-run 3 on, that start was his instruction to go, not a paste in chat. The agents start their runners once the gates are facts.

## Start gates

1. **Mini node synced.** Existing TN10 kaspad, existing data, no wipe. `isSynced` true, DAA moving. If that kaspad is down, start it from its existing data. Do not wipe.
2. **Desk node synced.** Existing private TN10 kaspad, existing data, loopback only, started with `pwsh` (PowerShell 7), never Windows PowerShell 5.1. No wipe. Synced, DAA moving. Do not start `KaspaPublicNode`. Leave the desk devnet alone. A pre-run does not stop the desk node process.
3. **No other runner.** No earlier runner, waiter, or counter on any machine. Lane above the abort line (2,500 coins and 18,000 usable tKAS). **The desk's private candidate list is present and not empty** (`HELD candidates-missing` otherwise). That open check was locked after pre-run 8 (the first open of that run read a hold when the private folder had no candidates file).
4. **Hashes first.** `hashes/SIXTEEN.md` with sixteen rows and an On origin line, from that repo's sixteen-family publisher. Genesis launch paths begin `launch::storm-2026-10-`. A hash is never invented. The old 8- or 32-family publisher is not used for this rehearsal.
5. **Open.** The desk pushes `logs/PRE-RUN-OPEN.md`. Mini then pushes `logs/MINI-ARMED.md`. Mini does not publish `MINI-ARMED` unless its node is synced with DAA moving.
6. **Counter, families, senders.** The desk runner starts the counter on its private node and waits for its start line (`HELD counter-down` otherwise). Then the genesis from the bundle that wrote `hashes/SIXTEEN.md`, the families, and the payment steps.

## Lines that stay, from the pre-runs

These are the worked lines we carry forward. The lock files in the pre-run repos are the source.

- A runner is never restarted mid-run to apply a scoring or tooling fix. The run is left alone and the fix goes into a later repo. If a runner must ever be replaced, its children are started detached so they survive the parent. (Pre-run 2.)
- A family STALL (no hop of that family landing for a while) is logged and is not dirt for HOLD scoring. (Pre-run 2.)
- The desk runner is rehearsed offline through HOLD-1 scoring, with faked logs and no live send, before a live run. A dry start that only reaches the gate check is not a rehearsal. (Pre-run 3.)
- Kept public nodes are unique kaspads, not unique hostnames. Two hostnames with one node id count as one node and get the senders for one kaspad. The bot's free node must be a different kaspad, read in the same pass. If none is free and synced, the bot writes a legal `NO SEND` and `no-free-node`. In this rehearsal only two unique public kaspads answered our checks, so HOLD-4 was a `NO SEND` from pre-run 5 through pre-run 15. A third kaspad is used only if it answers synced with its own node id. (Pre-runs 4–15.)
- After a payment-sender repair, the missing senders are actually started. The runner waits until the stopped senders are gone, takes them off its list, starts the ones that are missing, and checks that each one logged its start. (Pre-run 4.)
- A failed push of a HOLD file or of `logs/FAILED.md` is retried. A FAILED file is written locally first. If origin still refuses, the operator publishes the local file. (Pre-run 4.)
- A payment sender that restarts mid-run must not take its pool from the confirmed UTXO set alone while its chains are in the mempool. The repair hands pools off; the deal total must not exceed the preflight pile; the even split is checked after a reload. A payment ceiling is read against the pile size before it is called a node or network limit. (Pre-runs 5–6.)
- A family sender's mempool skip must clear when the node's mempool falls back under the line, or the skip must not apply to the families. A skip is logged. In pre-run 6 a one-way `mempool_90k` skip stopped all sixteen families from 20:31Z and made RETURN read not met even though the payments were on target. (Pre-run 6, fixed for pre-run 7.)
- Three payment senders per kept unique kaspad, once the pile is large enough that the cap has moved off the pile. (Pre-run 7.)
- The desk's private TN10 node is the inclusion counter when Mini cannot keep up. The desk node is not stopped for that count. (Pre-runs 7–15.)
- Mini's second read counts one hop per transaction (`classify()` is one row per transaction, not one per covenant output), and still dedupes by id and takes a removed chain block out of a minute still open. Checked offline, then live in pre-run 10 (49 of 50 cells in HOLD-1 to HOLD-4) pre-run 11 (155 of 155) pre-run 12 (259 of 265; read Mini's late-removal lines next to any mismatch) pre-run 13 (545 of 545), pre-run 14 (422 of 425, next to 42 late removals) and pre-run 15 (365 of 365). The desk counter stays the inclusion figure. (Pre-run 9 found the double count; pre-run 10 applied the fix.)
- Offers and fee cohorts are chosen for the question of that pre-run and are locked in that repo before the arm. Pre-run 9 used HOLD-1 1,013, HOLD-2 1,800, HOLD-3 2,500, then dropped to 1,013 as soon as HOLD-3 was scored, so the over-capacity stretch stayed short. Pre-run 10 used HOLD-1 1,013, HOLD-2 1,500, HOLD-3 1,800, then the same drop, with no over-capacity step. In that run the cheap chained tiers were already slow at HOLD-1, unlike pre-run 9, so a next repo that wants a clean 1,500 vs 1,800 reading needs a HOLD-1 like pre-run 9's, or a recorded reason why it is not. Pre-run 11 used a no-load BASE, HOLD-1 400, HOLD-2 700, HOLD-3 1,013, then RETURN at 0 (observed, not scored), and found that recorded reason in its mempool read (FINDINGS items 33 and 34). Scoring stays three clean minutes of submit-OK, or `NOT MET` after one repair. Inclusion is reported next to it and is not the score.
- Read the mempool before calling a slowdown an offer effect. A read-only probe on the counting node (mempool size, ready set and its mass, other traffic by fee rate, the node's own template) runs before the open and through the run; it submits nothing and no sender reads it. In pre-run 11 the floor's speed followed the ready mass (cut at about 9 blocks in that run) and another sender's same-fee heavy stream more than our payment offer, and our payments at 150 grew that stream's backlog, which then held the floor back for about 11 minutes after our offer went to 0. A no-load BASE and a RETURN at 0 in the same run are the references. (Pre-run 11.)
- Compare fees per family, on one node and one build, against a stretch with no outside competitor. In pre-run 12 BASE already had the outside stream in it; the ten stream-absent minutes were the clean reference, and with them all five tiers (100 to 200) ran the same. With the stream present the tiers separated in fee order but by much less than the (fee/mass)^3 weights, and only 200 stayed near its free-running rate most minutes. If the question needs the competitor on demand, use a bounded heavy lane of our own, with the operator's OK, capped by ready count and by the lane budget, stepped to zero at the end, and recorded publicly, because it also delays other users. (Pre-run 12.)
- Take a transaction's mass from the node, not from the SDK alone. Our SDK's `calculateStorageMass` counts every UTXO at plurality 1; consensus counts outputs with a covenant id (or a long script) at 2, four times the storage term, and the SDK's 100,000 standard-mass guard is not the node's per-transaction cap (the block limits). A heavy lane is calibrated by reading its first transaction's storage mass back from the desk mempool before the second goes out. (Pre-run 12, FINDINGS item 37.)
- A runner's labels (commit messages, FAILED text) take the pre-run number from the config, not from a copied string. (Pre-run 12 published its METHOD under `Pre-run 11 method.`.)
- Price a covenant hop on the node's mass, and calibrate one hop on the node before the open. In pre-run 13 our hops paid 13,046 × the tier rate but carried about 40,900 storage mass, so the node rated them at about 32 / 35 / 40 / 48 / 65 instead of 100 / 110 / 126 / 150 / 200 (FINDINGS item 40). Read a tier's rate as the node's `fee / max(storage, compute, normalized transient)`, not the builder's label. (Pre-run 13.)
- A heavy competitor of our own is a dial, not a guess: calibrate its first transaction on the node, step it by ready count, keep its submit cap in mind (at 600 a minute and one heavy per block, N = 60 held only about 44), step it to zero at the end, and state publicly that it slows other users' low-fee transactions while it runs. In pre-run 13 N = 10 barely mattered, N = 20 cost cheap tiers about a third, N = 40 and over cost them most of their rate, and the tiers recovered in the first minute at N = 0. (Pre-run 13, FINDINGS item 39.)
- Gate the family lane on usable tKAS, and consolidate key 0 before the open. Hop change fragments key 0 into coins under 3 tKAS; in pre-run 13 the coin count fell under 2,500 with 109,513 usable tKAS left and failed the run 1.5 minutes before RETURN. "Usable tKAS" over coins above 3 tKAS also understates key 0 (219,195 tKAS in all), so it is not a spend figure. Budget is not a limit from pre-run 14 (operator, 12 Nov); spend stays logged. (Pre-run 13.)
- The bot waiter's fetch must not hang past its pin: `timeout -k`, git low-speed limits, and a free check that comes after the pinned minute is written as late. In pre-run 13 one `timeout 25 git fetch` hung 336.7 s. (Pre-run 13.) Pre-run 14 ran with that fix and the waiter read HOLD-3 5 s after origin. The box that runs the bot can pause while its agent is idle (up to about 2 minutes in pre-run 14); keep it busy through HOLD-3 and the pin. (Pre-runs 13–14.)
- Read a tier against the competitor's rate, not its label. In pre-run 14, with hops priced on the node's mass, a hop at the heavy lane's rate kept 76–88 percent of BASE, about 1.6–2 times the lane's rate 96–104, and 2.4–3 times 101–106 (FINDINGS item 44). A defender who wants to keep its pace against a heavy flood pays about twice the flood's rate, as we observed it on one node. (Pre-run 14.)
- Keep a middle N = 0 control between competitor steps. When an outside stream is present, BASE can be lighter than the later N = 0 minutes (pre-run 14: 10.9 against 22–30 blocks of ready mass); give shares of both. (Pre-run 14.)
- Our lane's ready-mass hold (45 blocks) counts the outside stream's ready heavies too; in pre-run 14 it capped N = 40 (15 holds of 20 s). If a bigger lane is the question, raise the hold on purpose and say so. (Pre-run 14.) Pre-run 15 raised it to 80 on purpose; it never tripped. (Pre-run 15.)
- About twice the competitor's rate, again. In pre-run 15, with our lane uncapped at 126 and 150, a hop at about 2–2.4 times the lane's rate kept 95–99 percent of BASE and 3 times 98–103; at about 60 blocks of ready mass about three times was needed to hold fully (FINDINGS item 47). (Pre-runs 14–15.)
- Run the bot's free check once, read-only, before arming, and keep its libraries in its own folder. In pre-run 15 the box that runs the bot rebooted mid-run, its files rolled back, and the check crashed at the pin on a missing library; the file said `NO SEND check-failed`, pushed late (FINDINGS item 50). A check that did not read a node is never written as `no-free-node`. (Pre-run 15.)
- Family keys are funded again before a next repo publishes a new hash file. Pre-run 12 funded keys 1–15 again before hashes (15 transactions at 10:06:32Z on 12 Nov). After that run, status.mjs at about 11:53Z read that keys 1–15 had no genesis coin left (lane 5,480 coins, 174,299 usable tKAS, above the abort line). Pre-run 11 used about 23,700 usable tKAS of lane over 22,383 family hops; pre-run 12 about 41,350 over 40,440 (about 1.02 per hop, re-funding included). Read the lane against the next run's size: at pre-run 12's length the lane covers about three to four more runs before the abort line. Pre-run 13 funded keys 1–15 again at 12:13:14Z; after it, keys 1–15 had no genesis coin left and the coin count, not the tKAS, ended the run (see the line above on usable tKAS).

## When something breaks

Append a worked fix to that pre-run's `locks/LOCKED.md` in the form that page shows, and a repair line to `logs/FIXES.md` if the runner uses it. If the run dies before RETURN, write `logs/FAILED.md`, stop every runner, waiter, and counter, and do not resume. The next attempt is a new repo that starts from that repo's lock file. The result, or the miss, is also written into this map ([docs/NEURON.md](docs/NEURON.md)).
