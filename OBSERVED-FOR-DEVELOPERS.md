# What we observed, for developers and contributors (pre-runs 9–23)

Our intentions are good; our thought process may be questionable. These are notes from our own testing on Testnet-10, on our own nodes, mostly one desk node. Our methods may well be wrong, our readings of the code may be off, and none of this is advice or a claim about how Kaspa behaves in general. If you see a mistake, we would be glad to be corrected. Each line points to the numbered item in [FINDINGS.md](FINDINGS.md) with its source.

- **Submit-OK was not inclusion for us.** Under heavy payment load our desk could record a submit as OK while fewer of those payments landed in the same minutes (23–24).
- **Fee order seemed to matter once the ready set was larger than a block.** Under capacity our tiers landed alike; above it, higher sompi/gram landed more often, which looked consistent with our reading of the v2.1.0 selector's (fee/mass)^3 sampling. We may be reading the source wrong (25, 34).
- **Our own fee rates were not what we thought.** Our covenant hops were rated by the node on about three times the mass we priced them on, and our SDK's storage-mass helper read about four times low for one transaction shape. We fixed our pricing after that; the gap may be our misuse of the tools (37, 40, 43).
- **A heavy stream slowed cheaper transactions.** Our own heavy lane (about 266,700 mass a transaction) slowed our low-fee tiers and other TN10 users' transactions while it ran, and everything recovered within about a minute after it stopped. In pre-run 22 we could not see the effect in our node's other traffic, but our own low-fee stand-ins still lost up to about a quarter in the worst minute. In pre-run 23, with about 23 of ours ready at once, our low-fee stand-ins fell to about a tenth of their normal pace and other traffic on our node dropped below its usual range for two minutes. We are sorry for the slowdown to others (39, 44–49, 53, 58, 62, 66, 70, 74, 77–78).
- **Paying about twice a heavy stream's rate held up in our runs,** and about three times at the heaviest load we tried. That is a few short runs on one node (44, 47).
- **The node's own template sometimes left room unused** with many of our heavy transactions ready at a high rate, and stayed full with few of them at the same rate. In pre-run 22, with the submits paced, small templates still came more often when more of ours were ready at once. In pre-run 23 we held the number of ours ready while changing the number of chains, and the other way round: as far as we could see, the template followed how many of ours were ready (small templates in about 7, 30 and 88 percent of samples with 8, 15 and 30 ready), not the number of chains or the fee rate. With about 23 of ours ready, the template often held one of our heavy transactions and little else while hundreds of small ones waited. Our guess about why rests on our reading of the sampler, which we have not checked properly yet; it is not proven, one node, short segments, and our landing rate rose with the ready count too (41, 65, 68, 73, 75, 77).
- **Fee rules between our own lanes:** a rule that could step down settled every time we tried it; a rule that never stepped down kept climbing, even with a target it could reach. In pre-run 22 a never-down rule went all the way to our 2,000 cap; our own cap guard and freeze stopped it as designed, but the rule climbed straight back after the reset. Both sides were always ours, on one node (51–52, 56, 59–60, 63–64, 67, 71).
- **A sender limited by its own chain count did not land more by paying more.** With 8 chains, each waiting for its last transaction to leave the mempool, our lane landed about the same at 150, 460 and 2,000 sompi/gram, so the higher rate only cost more (about 4.2 times a landed transaction). The small dip at 2,000 in pre-run 22 did not repeat in pre-run 23. That is our own lane design, not a node property (72, 76).
- **The node's fee estimate rose with our heavy lane's rate** even when low-fee transactions were barely slowed, so it may overstate what a transaction needs in that situation (in pre-run 22 it read about 1,140 with our lane at 2,000, and in pre-run 23 about 1,090–1,310, against about 100–150 without it). In pre-run 23 it also drifted up over time at the same settings. A few runs, one node (69, 74, 79; pre-run 23: [its ANALYSIS](https://github.com/STP-KAS/tn10-storm-2026-11-11-prerun-23/blob/main/logs/ANALYSIS.md) and [LESSONS](https://github.com/STP-KAS/tn10-storm-2026-11-11-prerun-23/blob/main/logs/LESSONS.md)).
- **A KCC-20 mint line and covenant families on one node, light load:** in pre-run 24 all 30 of our one-at-a-time public mints were accepted next to four of our covenant families, and none of 910 of our accepted transactions was still in the node's mempool a minute or more later (the shape of issue #1146 was not seen at this load). One run, one node, very light load (81–82; [pre-run 24](https://github.com/STP-KAS/tn10-storm-2026-11-11-prerun-24)).
- **Lane carriers next to covenant families and mints, light load:** in pre-run 24 retry 1 all 8 carriers of one tic-tac-toe match (v1 transactions on the match's lane subnetwork) were accepted by our kaspad 2.1.0 TN10 node, with no reject, next to about 320 covenant hops a minute and a KCC-20 mint line ([repo](https://github.com/STP-KAS/tn10-storm-2026-11-11-prerun-24-retry-1)).

All runs were short, on Testnet-10 only, with our own coins and tools. Corrections, questions and better methods are welcome through issues on this repo.
